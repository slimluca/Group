# Dog Haven Group Editorial Style Guide

## Voice

Dog Haven Group should sound knowledgeable, calm, friendly, practical, global and honest about limitations.

Write for real dog owners who may be comparing countries, preparing for a puppy, planning travel, estimating costs or trying to understand where local advice belongs. The tone should be reassuring without becoming casual about safety, legal requirements or animal welfare.

## Language

Use international British English. Prefer forms such as organise, behaviour, travelling, personalised, judgement and neighbourhood unless quoting an official title or proper name that uses another spelling.

Use plain language and active voice where it reads naturally. Vary sentence length. Avoid exaggerated marketing language, keyword stuffing and repeated formulaic openings.

## Punctuation

Avoid unnecessary em dashes and semicolons in visitor-facing prose. Use commas and full stops naturally.

Preserve grammatically required compound hyphens such as first-time dog owner, dog-friendly accommodation, cross-border travel, evidence-based guidance, long-term planning, country-specific information, route-specific requirements and browser-based storage.

Preserve punctuation inside URLs, routes, filenames, slugs, data IDs, ISO values, date formats, source titles and code.

## Content

Every page should answer the visitor's question quickly, provide practical next steps and explain uncertainty clearly.

Changing or important claims should be tied to official or authoritative sources where appropriate. Never invent facts, false precision, fake reviews, fake veterinary approval, fake expert review or fake credentials.

Safety, health, legal and travel content should remain careful. General education can help readers ask better questions, but it must not replace qualified professional or official advice.

## Avoid

Avoid generic AI introductions, formulaic conclusions, repeated disclaimers, keyword stuffing, internal development terminology, fake expertise, fake reviews and fake veterinary approval.

Avoid phrases such as in today's world, navigating the complexities of, unlock, elevate, one-stop destination, valuable insights, comprehensive solution and it is important to note.

Avoid internal language such as scalable platform, content architecture, source registry, source-of-truth data, future phase, placeholder, production-ready, content cluster and internal hand-off.
