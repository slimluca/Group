# Dog Haven Group Global Growth Blueprint

Status: internal strategy document  
Public visibility: none; do not route, index, add to navigation, or include in the sitemap  
Planning horizon: phased, evidence-led growth

## Strategic principle

Dog Haven Group is the global, cross-border layer of the Dog Haven network. It should help a reader compare countries, prepare for international movement, understand methodology, use global planning tools, and then reach the correct local platform when the question becomes country-specific.

Dog Haven Group must not duplicate country-site content. Dog Haven Group should link visitors to the correct local platform when a question becomes country-specific. New public pages should exist only when they solve a real user need with original, supportable information; an intended future feature is not sufficient reason to publish a page.

## Platform responsibilities

### Dog Haven South Africa

Owns practical South African dog ownership:

- emergency guidance;
- health;
- ownership costs;
- local services;
- provinces and cities;
- dog-friendly places;
- adoption; and
- South African tools.

Group content may provide global context and link into this platform, but should not reproduce its local guidance.

### Dog Haven USA

Owns practical United States dog ownership:

- health;
- gear;
- national travel;
- state and city coverage;
- local services; and
- American tools.

Group content should hand off state-, city-, service-, product-, and US-rule-specific questions to this platform.

Group content should supply cross-border context, then link to this platform for Italian detail.

### Dog Haven Group

Owns global and cross-border dog ownership:

- international dog travel;
- moving abroad with a dog;
- country comparison;

## Editorial hand-off model

1. Answer the genuinely global part of the question.
2. State where requirements, costs, services, or lived conditions vary locally.
3. Link to current official sources when a changing rule is material.
4. Link to the relevant Dog Haven country platform for local depth.
5. Do not copy that local platform's explanation back into Group content.

## Prioritised platform roadmap

No phase below authorises a public placeholder page. Each phase requires a complete research, product, QA, privacy, and maintenance plan before release.

### Phase A — Dog Haven Global Passport Planner

Purpose: create a personalised international dog-travel preparation plan.

Future inputs:

- origin and destination country;
- travel date;
- dog age;
- travel method; and
- temporary travel or permanent relocation.

Future outputs:

- preparation timeline;
- document checklist;
- veterinary preparation questions;
- transport and crate preparation;
- arrival and return planning;
- official-source links; and
- printable plan.

Product rules: never invent laws or provide guarantees. Every rules-based output needs date-checked, official-source verification and a clear scope and freshness signal.

### Phase B — Dog Haven Global Dog Ownership Index

Compare ownership costs, rental and housing considerations, dog-friendly transport, public spaces, climate, veterinary access, international travel complexity, and everyday dog lifestyle.

Publish the methodology and cite the underlying data. Never invent scores. Label measured information separately from editorial interpretation, explain weighting and missing data, and make revisions reproducible.

### Phase C — Global Dog Cost Explorer

Enable country and cost-category comparison in local currency, with optional currency conversion, monthly and yearly planning, source dates, and transparent assumptions. Exchange-rate timing, geographic coverage, sample basis, inclusions, and exclusions must remain visible.

### Phase D — Moving Abroad Route Guides

Initial research candidates:

- South Africa to United Kingdom;
- South Africa to Australia;
- South Africa to United States;
- United States to Italy;
- United States to United Kingdom;
- Italy to United Kingdom; and
- Italy to United States.

Build only individually researched routes; do not mass-generate country combinations. Require official links, retain last-verification information internally, assign an owner, and operate a scheduled and event-triggered update workflow.

### Phase E — Global Dog-Friendly City Comparison

Compare apartment lifestyle, climate, transport, green space, travel access, rental considerations, everyday routines, and veterinary planning. Define comparable measures before selecting cities and avoid presenting tourism signals as evidence of resident quality of life.

### Phase F — Dog Travel Document Vault

Begin with browser-local storage, no account requirement, and no sensitive document upload to the server. Support reminders, checklist progress, and an emergency information card. Complete threat modelling, retention explanations, export/deletion controls, and accessibility QA before launch.

### Phase G — Dog Climate and Journey Planner

Educational inputs may include dog age, size, coat type, flat-faced or non-flat-faced anatomy, journey duration, expected climate, and transport type. The planner may surface preparation questions and risk factors; it must not diagnose health or state that a route is medically safe. Professional veterinary assessment remains separate.

### Phase H — Dog Haven Global Safety Center

Develop lost-dog preparation, an emergency contact card, evacuation checklist, disaster readiness, a travel emergency pack, emergency veterinary preparation, and printable resources. Local emergency numbers and procedures require jurisdiction-specific sourcing and clear hand-offs.

### Phase I — Dog Haven Data Hub

Publish original comparison tables, methodology, a source library, charts, downloadable reports, and public datasets where licensing and privacy permit. Maintain data dictionaries, provenance, versioning, correction notes, and accessible non-chart alternatives.

### Phase J — Dog Haven Daily

Develop a professional daily dog quiz, breed challenge, country challenge, dog-name challenge, knowledge streak, local browser badges, and weekly challenge. Engagement mechanics must remain useful and credible rather than childish, manipulative, or a substitute for substantive platform content.

## SEO and publishing principles

- Useful content before page count.
- No thin country pages, mass-generated city pages, or doorway pages.
- No duplicated country-site content or generic AI filler.
- No keyword stuffing.
- No fake statistics, rankings, expert reviews, or veterinary review.
- No invented laws or unsupported travel requirements.
- No public unfinished pages.

Every future major page requires:

- a defined search intent and user outcome;
- a unique title and description;
- exactly one H1 and a logical H2 hierarchy;
- useful original information;
- natural internal links and a country-platform hand-off where relevant;
- descriptive image alt text;
- only accurate, appropriate schema;
- visible sources where needed; and
- an owner, review cadence, source date, and update process for changing information.

## Release gates

A phase may move toward public release only when it has:

1. a validated user need and scope;
2. authoritative sources and a citation policy;
3. original value beyond existing country sites;
4. working functionality or complete substantive content;
5. privacy, accessibility, mobile, and failure-state review;
6. SEO metadata and schema review;
7. an update owner and maintenance cadence; and
8. no public routes for incomplete sub-features.
